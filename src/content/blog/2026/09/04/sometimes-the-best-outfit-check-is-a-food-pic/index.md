Let’s break down the outfit check - it’s a polished picture, vibrant, maybe has an interesting element of texture or perhaps even a little sexy. Now think about the last proper food pic you took - it’s a polished picture, vibrant, maybe has an interesting element of texture or perhaps even a little sexy. More and more, it seems the food picture is not only replacing the outfit check, but it is starting to seem even better.

This phrase brings to mind Iris Law’s cookbook from COVID, which she created with Lila Moss to raise money for the NHS. What we got? Iris Law cosplaying a blueberry - because food is the outfit. Or if you have stumbled upon @lvebriaaa turning runway looks into sexy dishes - again looking at the dish has replaced the value of the original fashion look.

Let’s face it: The curation of a good dish is becoming as hot as haute couture.

Food and fashion have both unfortunately always carried high value and defined class. Looking back at the 1807, ‘Rôti Sans Pareil’ is a dish that includes 17 layers of different animals, each one inside another. Or the 6 to 10 layers that made up the full look of women of the aristocracy, from whale bone support to decorative petticoats. When it comes to food and fashion, gluttony and abundance, skill and technique, glamour and finish, all distinguish our wealth from someone else’s.

Today, however, it seems that people are less extravagant at showing wealth - with quiet luxury covering all the bases. It’s the hints of if you know, you know that now divide classes. Currently, it is the idea that if your lifestyle reflects good quality - you are probably extremely wealthy. For clothing, it is not only a focus on designer but also on fabric quality, personalization and expense, but on good quality fabric. The same is now true of food. If your meals are strong, healthy, organic - you are not only flaunting your accessibility to nutrition but your ability to sustain a lifestyle of good, expensive food.

Well I guess the question now is, if these are becoming so inaccessible, then why would a food picture be the next best thing?

Truthfully, good food is not limited to Erewhon, or a 500 pound dining experience. But it can be found in street vendors in Vietnam, small shops in India, your grandparent’s house in Argentina or your parent’s garden in rural Bulgaria. Unfortunately, fashion is still yet to get to a global standard of being ‘good’ everywhere. Clothes are more and more monopolized by a world large industry, but food can come from all corners and families around the world.

Hence why sometimes the best outfit check is a food pic. Because the way that food connects us, shapes us and shows who we are, the things we are excited to try, and the global citizens we are, is more telling than a picture of someone in a mass-produced T-shirt.

Not only this, but people’s outfit checks are curated to show aspects of their personality. However, if everyone is sporting vibram shoes, patterned tights, UO yoga pants, turquoise, and rhinestones - then what exactly is their personality? Of course this is not always true. But for the influencers dominating the outfit check domain - they are more or less imitating each other. And ultimately it’s becoming exhausting to see the same variations of trends on every single fashion girl’s instagram.

And when one aspect becomes oversaturated, people always want a new version of individuality and inspiration. Hence what we eat. No two dishes on instagram are made the same, no two people have the same flavour palettes. What we eat comes from ourselves, our cultures, our friends’ cultures and the times we tried a new dish or the time we ate at that tiny restaurant never to be seen again. At times when people are sick of the fashion trends, sick of the similarities in everyone’s outfits, we are in dire need of a little individuality.
